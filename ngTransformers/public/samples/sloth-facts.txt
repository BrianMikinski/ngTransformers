Sloth facts

Sloths are tree-dwelling mammals from the rainforests of Central and South America. There are two groups: two-toed sloths and three-toed sloths. Both have three claws on their back feet; the name refers to the front feet.

Sloths are famously slow. They move through the canopy at a few metres per minute and spend most of their lives hanging upside down from branches. Their slowness is a strategy, not a flaw: a very low metabolic rate lets them live on a diet of leaves that provides little energy.

A sloth's stomach has several chambers, and digesting a single meal can take weeks. Sloths climb down to the ground only about once a week, to relieve themselves, which is when they are most exposed to predators.

Their fur grows in the opposite direction to most mammals, so rain runs off while they hang upside down. Algae grow in grooves in the fur, giving many sloths a greenish tint that works as camouflage. Moths and beetles live in the fur too.

Surprisingly, sloths are good swimmers. They can slow their heart rate and hold their breath for up to forty minutes underwater.

In the wild, sloths sleep around nine to ten hours a day. In captivity they often sleep much longer.
